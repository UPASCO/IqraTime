Frequently asked questions

The things people ask us most about IqraTime.

Is IqraTime really free?

Yes, completely free and ad-free — and that will not change. There is no subscription, no paid feature, and no account to create. Donations are entirely optional and unlock nothing.

Does the app work without an internet connection?

Yes. The corpus, the translations, and the notification scheduling all ship inside the app. Once installed, IqraTime needs no connection at all for day-to-day use.

Is any of my personal data collected?

None whatsoever. No account, no server, no analytics, no tracker, no advertising identifier. Your favorites, history, and settings never leave your phone.

Which languages is IqraTime available in?

12 languages: Arabic, French, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Bengali, Simplified Chinese, Italian, Russian, Dutch, and German — with full right-to-left support for Arabic.

Can I choose what time I receive notifications?

Yes, entirely. You set the active hours, the days of the week, the frequency (from once an hour to once every 12 hours), fixed times if you prefer, and an automatic night silence.

Are there hadith as well as Quran verses?

Yes. Alongside the āyāt, you can enable authentic hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Every āyah also has a tafsir explaining its meaning and context, one tap away.

What is the “Āyah of the day”?

Every day IqraTime highlights one āyah that is identical for all users, chosen deterministically from about 165 of the most recognized āyāt — with no server: the computation happens on your phone. Two devices show the same āyah on the same day, which makes it perfect for group sharing. It changes at local midnight and doesn't return until the full cycle is complete.

How does memorization (hifz) work?

You add an āyah to your memorization list, and the app schedules reviews at growing intervals: 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 then 60 days. At each review the reference is shown, you recite from memory, reveal the text and rate yourself — the app never judges your recitation, it only schedules. A lapse brings the āyah back to the start of the cycle, and a mastered āyah keeps being maintained every 60 days.
